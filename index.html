An Introduction of Quantum Computation

Quantum computation is a model of computation based on the principles of quantum mechanics. Unlike classical computers, which operate on bits, quantum computers operate on quantum bits, or qubits, allowing them to take advantage of quantum phenomena like superposition, entanglement, and interference. This naturally motivates us to ask an important question in complexity theory: can quantum computers solve certain problems more efficiently than classical computers? This question is especially relevant to the Strong Church–Turing Thesis, which claims that any physically feasible model of computation can be efficiently simulated by a probabilistic Turing machine. Investigating quantum computation allows us to explore the limitations of classical computational models and the possibility of more efficient quantum algorithms.

This website provides a mathematical introduction to quantum computation. We begin by introducing qubits and the mathematical representation of quantum states, including superposition, entanglement, and measurement. Then, we develop the quantum circuit model, introducing reversible computation, unitary transformations, and fundamental quantum gates. With this foundation, we can then explore quantum computational complexity, specifically the class $\mathsf{BQP}$, its relationships with classical complexity classes, and what it means for the Strong Church–Turing Thesis. Finally, we study Grover's algorithm. We provide both an intuitive explanation and a rigorous analysis of this algorithm.

This website is intended for students familiar with the material covered in 15-455, particularly Boolean circuits, Turing machines, and basic computational complexity. We also assume familiarity with linear algebra. No prior knowledge of quantum mechanics or its physics is required.
